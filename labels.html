Label guide

Read the front last.

Start with serving size, ingredients, added sugar, fiber, protein, sodium, and the claims that matter to your own goals.

Serving size

Compare the amount you actually eat with the labeled serving. Nutrition numbers only make sense in the context of that serving.

Ingredients

Ingredients are generally listed by weight. “Natural” does not automatically mean healthier, and an unfamiliar name does not automatically mean harmful.

Added sugar

Use the Nutrition Facts panel to compare similar products rather than relying on front-of-package marketing.

Compare what matters

Depending on your goals, compare fiber, protein, sodium, saturated fat, added sugar, serving size, and the complete ingredient list across similar foods.

Look for patterns, not verdicts

A label can describe a food. It cannot by itself diagnose symptoms or prove that one ingredient caused how you feel.
