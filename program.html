The full pathway

30 days to begin. 90 days to build sustainability.

The 30-day foundation is the most developed portion. Days 31–90 are organized as a transparent curriculum roadmap requiring final evidence and safety review before sale.

DAYS 1–7

Prepare and simplify

  • Reduce refined sugar, refined wheat, and heavily processed foods.
  • Increase water intake appropriately.
  • Begin daily walking or suitable movement.
  • Add pumpkin, flax, and chia seeds as tolerated.
  • Begin a food, symptom, sleep, and energy journal.
DAYS 8–14

Support digestion through food

  • Continue a whole-food-focused pattern.
  • Try tolerated fermented foods such as yogurt, kefir, or sauerkraut.
  • Learn the difference between probiotics, prebiotics, and fermented foods.
  • Continue hydration, movement, and journaling.
DAYS 15–21

Explore metabolic habits safely

  • Prioritize adequate protein and balanced meals.
  • Consider time-restricted eating only when appropriate and safe.
  • Prioritize sleep, recovery, stress management, and regular movement.
  • Optional sauna or baths are wellness practices—not detox cures.
DAYS 22–30

Review and maintain

  • Review the journal for patterns, not assumptions.
  • Keep habits that were helpful and tolerable.
  • Build a realistic maintenance plan.
  • Prepare questions for a clinician when symptoms or data need evaluation.

Days 31–90 curriculum direction

Days 31–60

Continue and personalize

  • Plant diversity, fiber variety, and affordable meal planning
  • Label-reading practice and added-sugar comparisons
  • Sleep, hydration, movement, stress, and recovery patterns
  • Fermented foods and tolerance review
  • Supplement quality education and interaction cautions
  • Digestive conditions that can resemble “dysbiosis,” parasites, or Candida
  • Questions for objective testing and clinician review
Days 61–90

Maintain and deepen understanding

  • Sustainable routines and relapse planning
  • Metabolic health education: prevention, improvement, and remission language
  • International food-additive and public-health comparisons
  • Evidence literacy: established, emerging, conflicting, and unknown
  • Personal maintenance plan and future goals
  • Optional support, coaching, or University learning
Important: Detailed day-by-day Days 31–90 materials are not yet represented as finalized or medically reviewed. This page preserves the agreed direction without making a false completion claim.

Daily foundation elements

Hydration

Water needs vary. Mineral salt water requires safety qualifications and is not appropriate for everyone.

Whole foods

Emphasize affordable foods and reduce reliance on heavily processed products.

Protein and fiber

Build meals around adequate nourishment, plant diversity, and individual tolerance.

Movement

Use suitable daily activity, balance, strength, walking, and recovery.

Sleep

Support regular sleep timing and reduce habits that interfere with recovery.

Journal

Track patterns without diagnosing yourself from a single reaction.

Food education

Understand ingredients, serving sizes, added sugar, sodium, and alternatives.

Appropriate care

Use qualified medical evaluation and objective testing when needed.
