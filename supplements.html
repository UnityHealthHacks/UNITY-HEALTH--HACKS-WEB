9.5 DEVELOPMENT — TEST / REVIEW ONLY. Evidence-first educational content; not public release authority.

Learning topic

Forms, quality, and safety

Supplement education should separate nutritional need, product form, evidence, interactions, and marketing claims.

Supplements are optional tools, not substitutes for food or medical care. Need, dose, form, interactions, and testing matter.

TopicQuestions the site should teach
Vitamin B12Cyanocobalamin, methylcobalamin, hydroxocobalamin, and adenosylcobalamin; conversion, absorption, cost, clinical significance, and unsupported population claims. Do not imply methylcobalamin is universally superior.
FolateFood folate, folic acid, and 5-MTHF/methylfolate; avoid assuming methylated forms are necessary for everyone.
MineralsMagnesium, zinc, iron, and interactions; testing or clinician review when appropriate.
Vitamin D and omega-3sDiet, measured need, dose, quality, and interaction considerations.
Probiotics/prebioticsStrain, product quality, individual tolerance, uncertain universal benefits, and food-based options.
Digestive enzymesSpecific indications, tolerance, and why routine use is not automatically necessary.
Amino acidsProtein adequacy first; product quality and individual goals.
Activated charcoal/bindersActivated charcoal has specific medical uses in selected poisonings, but it is not a routine “detox” tool. It can bind substances in the gut, interfere with medicines, and carries risks; possible poisoning should be handled with poison-control or emergency guidance rather than a wellness protocol.
Medication safety: Supplements and binders can interact with medicines. Product marketing should never replace interaction review.

Evidence references: NIH ODS — Vitamin B12; NIH ODS — Folate; Poison Control — Activated Charcoal. Review date: August 30, 2026.
